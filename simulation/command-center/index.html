How It Works

The simulator, the probability models, and the reflective loop.

A The simulator: real lnd code, hidden liquidity

routing/sim_graph.go is an in-memory Lightning network with hidden per-channel balances and per-direction policies (base + ppm fees, CLTV deltas, min/max HTLC). It implements lnd's Graph interface, so lnd's production pathfinding and mission control run against it unmodified.

SendHtlc enforces real forwarding semantics — FeeInsufficient, IncorrectCltvExpiry, AmountBelowMinimum, TemporaryChannelFailure (a liquidity miss) — updating balances and unwinding on failure. Topologies are synthetic (line, grid, hubspoke, smallworld) or loaded from a describegraph mainnet snapshot; hidden balances follow a half, uniform, or bimodal model, the last hugging one end of each channel — the realistic, hard regime.

The CLI cmd/routesim takes a params JSON plus a scenario file and emits per-attempt traces and aggregate metrics. It is fast: ten payments on a 200-node net in ~0.3 s including process startup, so thousands of evaluations are cheap.

B Mission control & the two probability estimators

Pathfinding is Dijkstra over a cost roughly equal to fees + timelock_penalty + attempt_cost / P(route): the virtual attempt cost (100 sat + 1000 ppm by default) divided by the estimated success probability, so unreliable routes look expensive. P(route) is the product of per-hop probabilities from one of two estimators, fed by mission control, which records the last success/fail amount and timestamp for every directed node pair.

Apriori estimator

default

Every unknown hop starts at 0.6, scaled down as the amount approaches channel capacity. Observations override the prior — a pair that just relayed ≥ amt gets 0.95; one that failed at ≤ amt drops to ~0, healing back over a 1h half-life. The clever bit is node-level extrapolation: a node's failures on other channels drag down all its channels via a weighted average (weight 0.5) — one bad channel makes the whole node suspect.

Bimodal estimator

opt-in · models where liquidity sits

Assumes balance density ~ e^(−x/s) + e^((x−c)/s) — funds parked at one end or the other (s = 300k sat). Each attempt narrows a per-channel [can-send, cannot-send] window: success at a proves ≥ a, failure at b proves < b, relaxing back to [0, capacity] over a 7-day decay. Probability is the mass above amt within that window; node-level info mixes in (weight 0.2) unless fresh direct info exists.

The MPP layer sits on top: if no single route clears MinProbability (1%), the session halves the amount and retries, down to a min shard and up to MaxParts shards.

C GEPA's reflective loop

The seed is lnd's own defaults (routesim --dump-defaults). On a fresh corpus it scores 0.27–0.58 with 30–63% success and up to 26 attempts per payment on the hard bimodal nets — plenty of failure for reflection to chew on, which is exactly what GEPA needs. A saturated seed would just be returned unchanged.

Each round, the reflection LM (openai/gpt-5.6-sol) reads the score plus compact failure traces, proposes a mutated params JSON, and the evaluator scores it. Generalization mode keeps the incumbent unless a candidate genuinely holds up across splits — so the curve climbs by keeping wins and discarding regressions, not by chasing val-set noise.

D Phase 3: a paradigm-free router, now evolving

Parameter tuning was deliberately the loop-validation step — we were never tied to that paradigm, and run1 confirmed it: the lnd defaults survived 400 evals unchanged, no knob beat them. The real target, now live, is a paradigm-free SimRouter interface in the sim: gossip view + local balances + attempt feedback in, a route out — so GEPA generates whole routing strategies in Go as candidates, with today's stack (and a hand-written 300-line seed router) as the baselines to beat.

The bar turned out to be the seed, not lnd: a naive cheapest-path router with per-payment failure blacklisting already beats production lnd pathfinding on the hard corpus (0.547 vs 0.393 objective, 2× the success rate at a third of the attempts). The overnight run, code_hard1, went on to clear that bar too: its evolved champion (hb1, 872 lines) beats both lnd and the seed on the sealed test set (0.586 vs 0.530 vs 0.309 objective) and on out-of-distribution corpus-v2 topologies it never trained on (0.545 vs 0.487 vs 0.357) — at 4–7× fewer attempts per payment (≈9 vs lnd's ≈50). From nothing but the objective and per-attempt failure traces, it independently arrived at an explicit bimodal liquidity prior — the same "funds sit at one end of the channel" hypothesis lnd's own bimodal estimator was analytically derived from — plus per-edge liquidity bounds with a confidence score and a risk-adjusted Dijkstra, well beyond the seed's simple blacklist.

The frontier didn't stop at hb1: code_hard1 went on to accept a sibling, hb2 (1166 lines), that Pareto-trades against it rather than replacing it — hb1 stays the hard-regime specialist (sealed test 0.586) while hb2 generalizes better out-of-distribution (0.577 vs hb1's 0.545), and both still beat lnd and the seed on every set. That makes this a genuine Pareto frontier of evolved routers, not a single champion.

code_hard1 has since finished: it terminated at 135 of its 400-eval budget when a pathological candidate hit an infinite loop and blew the subprocess timeout, which propagated and stopped the run (the harness now catches this and scores a hung candidate 0.0 instead of crashing, so a future run can use its full budget). GEPA's own valset selection had already settled on hb1 as the best program — val aggregate 0.31654, matching the independent hard-val measurement to four decimals — so hb1 is the definitive champion by both GEPA's own selection and the three-way validation above, with hb2 standing as its best out-of-distribution sibling.

A Fable code-reviewer agent independently audited the sandbox mid-run and found one critical hole: the graph session handed a candidate the concrete *SimGraph, type-assertable back to read hidden balances or rewrite liquidity outright. Fixed the same day — the session now only ever exposes the sealed view — and every in-flight candidate (code1, omni1) was checked against the escape: zero hits, so no result above is tainted.

A follow-up run, code_mix1, seeded GEPA directly from hb1 on a mixed hard/scale-free corpus to push further. Reflecting on an 872-line candidate mostly produces rejects — 12 proposals in a row before the first accept — and even that accept, mb1 (1306 lines, iteration 17), doesn't dominate: three-way validation puts it below hb1 on the hard sealed test (0.570 vs 0.586) and below both hb1 and hb2 on out-of-distribution corpus-v2 (0.529 vs 0.545 / 0.577). GEPA kept it on the Pareto front for per-example specialization, not aggregate gain — its own minibatch score (0.431) was below the seed's (0.519).

The run kept going, though, and the giant-seed reflection eventually paid off: by 314 of its 500-eval budget, code_mix1 had accepted two more Pareto siblings (1410 and 1525 lines), the last of which, mx_c3 (saved as champions/router_mx3_generalist_v1.go), is a genuine generalist champion. Three-way validation: hard sealed test hb1 0.586 / hb2 0.545 / mx_c3 0.583; OOD corpus-v2 hb1 0.545 / hb2 0.577 / mx_c3 0.581; combined average hb1 0.565 / hb2 0.561 / mx_c3 0.582. mx_c3 strictly dominates hb2 (wins both sets), ties hb1 on the hard test within noise, and posts the best combined average of any router evolved so far. Structurally it extends hb1's bimodal-prior-plus-liquidity-bounds core with an adaptive retry-at-lower-amount policy (candidateLowerRetryFactor) that pays off on the more diverse mixed corpus. Champions of record are now hb1 (hard-regime specialist) and mx_c3 (generalist, superseding hb2) — the "diminishing returns" read on the giant seed was premature, it just took ~300 more evals to cash out.

The inversion

Everything above — mission control, bimodal windows, apriori priors — stops being a constraint and becomes background knowledge the reflection prompt can draw on. The optimizer is free to keep it, reshape it, or throw it out, and it has already found something the paradigm didn't imagine.
